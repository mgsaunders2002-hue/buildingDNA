STAGE 05

Diagnose the DNA

You now know the major structural rules of DNA. In this stage, you will use those rules as evidence. Rather than simply identifying an incorrect structure, you will compare data, evaluate competing explanations, and defend a molecular diagnosis.

PART A — INTERPRET THE EVIDENCE

Which DNA sample should separate most easily?

A researcher examines three double-stranded DNA regions. Each region is the same length, but their base-pair composition differs.

Use only the information in the table to predict which sample should require the least energy to separate.

DNA Sample Total Base Pairs G–C Pairs A–T Pairs
Sample A 12 8 4
Sample B 12 3 9
Sample C 12 7 5

Based only on base-pair composition, which sample should separate most easily?

PART B — DIAGNOSE THE RESULT

The experimental result does not fully match the prediction.

The researcher heats the samples and records the following observation:

Experimental observation:

Sample C begins separating at a much lower temperature than expected from its G–C content alone.

The researcher proposes three possible explanations. Evaluate each one before choosing the explanation that best accounts for the unexpected result.

EXPLANATION A

Lower G–C content

Sample C separates unusually easily because it contains fewer G–C base pairs than Sample A.

EXPLANATION B

A mismatched base pair

Sample C contains a base mismatch that prevents one position from forming the normal complementary hydrogen-bond pattern.

EXPLANATION C

An extra phosphate group

Sample C contains one additional phosphate group in its sugar-phosphate backbone.

Which explanation best accounts for Sample C separating much more easily than expected?
